How to use it

  1. Put a record on the platter so the phone sits on vinyl, not on bare metal or the mat's ribs.
  2. Press Zero sensor with the phone lying still on the stopped platter. Two seconds.
  3. Press Start, lay the phone flat next to the spindle, and start the turntable.
  4. Give it a full minute. Wow and flutter need revolutions to show themselves.
  5. Stop the platter; the reading freezes. Save run keeps it in History.

The gyroscope measures how fast the whole platter turns, so it does not matter where on the record the phone sits, or whether it is centred. It does matter that the phone is flat and cannot slide.

Reading the numbers

  • Average is the honest speed figure: total angle turned divided by total time.
  • % is how far that is from the target. Cents is the same error as musical pitch — 17 cents is roughly the smallest shift most people can hear on a sustained note.
  • W&F peak is the largest departure from your own average; RMS is the typical one. Both are measured after smoothing, so they describe wow (slow wavering) rather than true flutter.
  • Once per rev isolates the part of the error that repeats exactly once every turn.

What this cannot do

Phone gyroscopes report about 60 readings a second, so anything wavering faster than ~30 times a second is invisible here — that is the flutter end of the band a 3,150 Hz test record measures. Treat the W&F figures as a wow measurement and a good relative indicator, not as a lab result. The noise floor of your own sensor is shown under the buttons after you zero it; readings near that floor are the phone, not the deck.

Compass calibration

Start the platter at any speed and let the phone ride for at least a minute. The compass counts whole revolutions; the gyroscope is then corrected to agree with it.
